dusk bot/glossary

what is an ai routine?

an ai routine is a job you describe once and have a bot repeat. a time or an event starts it.

by the dusk bot team · checked 20 november 2026 · how we made this

the useful part is the judgment you want each time, like spotting a shortage before a class. tell the bot what deserves your attention and when it can stay quiet.

in one line

an ai routine pairs a repeatable task with a time or event that tells the bot when to do it.

an example

florence counts bags of clay on thursdays. here's a sample routine for her studio.

“every thursday at four, compare the clay count in the stock tab of the studio sheet with friday's class list in the same sheet. tell me here if we're short. if the count is over a week old, ask for a fresh count. otherwise, stay quiet if we have enough.”

what makes silence useful?

missing information deserves its own answer. an empty shelf and a blank entry mean different things. give the routine an old count on purpose for its first run. it should flag the date. if it reports plenty of clay, fix that instruction before relying on it.

set the rhythm in dusk bot

text the job to your bot and read the next run on its card. the card lets you edit, pause or undo the routine. ask which time zone it uses if the hour looks odd.12

open the routine in the bot's side panel to see its recent runs. if you expected a shortage report and got silence, first look for a run at the right time.1

when a reminder is enough

use a calendar reminder if all you need is a nudge to count the clay. dusk bot earns its place when someone needs to compare the count with another piece of information. if nobody records stock, a routine has nothing reliable to compare.

questions

does a routine need a clock?

no. an event in a connected app can start a dusk bot routine too. choose the event when the work has to follow a change.1

what do i do when the class takes a break?

pause the routine on its card. when term starts again, review the next run and update the stock and class lists before you rely on another report.1
