dusk bot/routines

pick tomorrow’s first todoist task tonight

leave yourself a first move for tomorrow morning. at the end of the day, a bot reads unfinished work in one todoist project.

by the dusk bot team · checked 20 november 2026 · how we made this

it picks a starting task, keeps tomorrow’s timed commitments in view and sets older work aside for a decision. the plan lands in chat with your original dates intact.

the message

paste this into a chat with your bot

every evening at 6 pm, read the incomplete tasks in my todoist project called "work". include tasks overdue, still due today or due tomorrow.

in this chat, list tomorrow’s timed tasks in order. pick one starting task from the remaining items. use my marked priorities, then the earliest due date to break ties. give the task title and your reason. group the other items due today or earlier under “decide later”. flag anything else due tomorrow by name.

never change dates, priorities or completion status. stay quiet after a successful read with no qualifying tasks. tell me if you couldn't finish reading the project.
when
at 6 pm each evening, looking one calendar day ahead
needs
read-only todoist access to incomplete tasks in your chosen project3. replace "work" with your project name.

what comes back

an example, not a real run

thursday plan · print orders
timed: 11 am, collect the paper samples.
start with: price the binding for nell foster. highest marked priority, due thursday.
decide later: reconcile the postage receipts, due monday.

make it yours

  • plan around your last shift. move 6 pm to the time you usually close the project.
  • give clients their own lane. name a single client project if personal errands keep winning the first slot.
  • choose your tie-breaker. put an overdue promise ahead of tomorrow’s untimed work if that is how you choose by hand.

when it goes wrong

  • everything looks urgent. reserve the top priority for work you would actually interrupt your morning to do.
  • a due date turns into an appointment. show a clock time only when the task has one. leave date-only tasks untimed.

keep overdue work visible without letting it fill tomorrow

yesterday’s unfinished task still needs a decision. a proposed first move helps you start. moving the rest to tomorrow would hide how long those tasks have waited.

if your todoist list already opens with exactly what you need to do next, skip the extra evening message.

questions

does this fit tasks into gaps between meetings?

it reads todoist alone. a task due at noon tells you when it is due. your free time is a separate question.

what happens on friday evening?

tomorrow means saturday. if you work weekdays, ask for the next working day and spell out what friday should cover.
